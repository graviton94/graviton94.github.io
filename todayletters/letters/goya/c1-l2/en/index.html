Goya to Martín Zapater · April 1777

Letters from Goya · Chapter I Madrid and the Cartoons · Madrid

Listen to the first sentence (Spanish)

The letter and its translation

Infinitas gracias, y muchas más, por las expresiones de amistad que te merezco, y no dudes que, si había de cansar alguno, sería a ti.

Endless thanks, and many more, for the words of friendship I have from you; and don't doubt that if I had to trouble anyone, it would be you.

Pero, gracias a Dios, tengo, y con esperanzas de tener, como te insinué, campicos.

But, thank God, I have, and hope to have, as I hinted to you, a few little fields.

Pues, amigo, ya estoy bueno, gracias a Dios, que me he escapado de buena, etc.

Well, my friend, I'm well again now, thank God; I've had a narrow escape, etc.

Selected sentences from the letter. The translation is our own.

Curator’s note
In the spring of 1777 Goya was seriously ill; the letter does not say what the illness was. He wrote this to Martín once he had recovered. The same year he painted bright cartoons such as ‘The Parasol’. ‘Campicos’, little fields, stand for the quiet life at home he always dreamed of.
